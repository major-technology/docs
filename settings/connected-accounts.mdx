Some connectors use [per-user OAuth](/learn/connectors/overview#shared-vs-per-user-connections) - instead of a shared account, each person authorizes their own (for example, your own Google Calendar). The accounts you've authorized are managed from the **Connected Accounts** settings page.

## Managing your accounts

From **Connected Accounts** you can:

- **View** every third-party service you've connected.
- **Disconnect** an individual account.
- **Re-authenticate** an account whose credentials have expired.

You can also sign out of a personal connector from the **Connectors** page: a connector you're signed in to shows a **Sign out** button on its row, which opens the same disconnect dialog. If you're signed in on more than one environment for that connector, each sign-out disconnects one environment, and the button stays until the last one is disconnected.

When you disconnect an account, you lose access to any app or agent feature that relies on that connection until you reconnect it.

## Connecting an account

You usually don't visit this page to connect - apps prompt you when needed. You can also click **Sign in** on the connector's row on the **Connectors** page. If you open an app that uses a per-user connector and haven't authorized your account, Major redirects you to a `/connect` page to complete OAuth, then returns you to the app. Trying to invoke the connector before connecting returns an error asking you to finish authorizing.

Finish the provider's sign-in in the same browser where you're signed in to Major. Major saves the authorization only for the Major user who started it, so if you're signed out of Major, signed in as a different user, or your session expired partway through, the connection is refused and you'll be asked to sign in to Major as the user who started it. Start again and it will go through.

If the service later refuses your saved sign-in (for example, because you revoked access or the token expired), Major marks the account as needing to reconnect. The next time a Platform Agent chat uses the connector, it asks you to sign in again instead of failing with an error. A successful **Test Connection** clears the mark.
